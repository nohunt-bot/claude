What is a proforma invoice?

A proforma invoice is a preliminary bill you send before the work is done or the goods are delivered — essentially a detailed quote laid out like an invoice. It tells the client exactly what they'll be charged and on what terms, but it isn't a demand for payment and it doesn't go in your books. Here's what it really is, how it differs from a normal invoice, and when a freelancer or small business actually needs one.

The short definition

"Proforma" is Latin for "as a matter of form." A proforma invoice is a good-faith estimate presented in invoice format: it lists the items or services, quantities, prices, taxes, and terms, so the buyer can see the full cost up front. What makes it proforma rather than a real invoice is timing and intent — it's issued before the sale is finalised, and it does not create a debt or get recorded as revenue.

Think of it as the bridge between a casual quote and a formal invoice. A quote says "this is roughly what it'll cost." A proforma says "this is the exact bill you'll receive — confirm and we'll proceed."

Proforma invoice vs a real (commercial) invoice

They look almost identical, which is why they're so often confused. The difference is what each one does:

Proforma invoiceFinal (commercial) invoice
When it's sentBefore delivery or before work startsAfter delivery, or at agreed billing point
Is it a payment demand?No — it's an estimate to confirmYes — it requests payment by a due date
Legally binding?Not binding; figures can still changeBinding record of a sale owed
Goes in your accounts?No — not recorded as income or a receivableYes — recorded as revenue / accounts receivable
Invoice numberOften marked "Proforma," may use a draft refSequential, official invoice number

The headline rule: a proforma is not yet a bill you owe. When the client confirms and the work or shipment proceeds, you replace it with a proper, numbered invoice that is the legal request for payment. For the wider family of documents, see the difference between an invoice and a receipt.

When would you actually use one?

Most one-person businesses can go years without sending a proforma. But there are specific moments where it's exactly the right tool:

  • Requesting a deposit. A proforma is the classic way to show a client the full project cost and the upfront amount due before they commit — see how to ask for a deposit.
  • The client needs cost approval first. Larger clients often require a document to raise a purchase order or release budget. A proforma gives their finance team a firm number to sign off without you having booked the revenue.
  • International shipping & customs. Proforma invoices are standard for declaring the value of goods to customs before a final invoice exists.
  • Prepayment or "pay before we ship." When you want money up front, a proforma states the amount and terms so the buyer can pay, after which you issue the real invoice or receipt.
  • Scope still being finalised. When the exact figure might shift, a proforma signals "this is the expected cost, not yet final."

What to put on a proforma invoice

A proforma carries almost all the same fields as a normal invoice — the goal is for the client to see the real, final-looking cost. Include:

  • The word "Proforma" clearly in the title or near the invoice number, so no one mistakes it for a payment demand.
  • Your name/business and contact details, and the client's.
  • An issue date and, ideally, a "valid until" date — proforma prices shouldn't be open-ended.
  • A clear line-item breakdown: description, quantity, unit price, and total.
  • Any taxes (e.g. VAT/sales tax) shown the same way they'll appear on the final invoice.
  • Payment terms — deposit required, or terms like Net 30 — and how you'd like to be paid.
  • A short note that it's not a demand for payment, e.g. "This proforma is for confirmation only; a final invoice will follow."

How to turn a proforma into a real invoice

Once the client confirms and the work or shipment goes ahead, the proforma's job is done. To convert it:

  1. Create the final invoice with the same line items, updated for any agreed changes.
  2. Give it a proper sequential invoice number (the one you'll record in your books).
  3. Remove the "Proforma" label and add a real due date and payment terms.
  4. Note any deposit already paid and show the remaining balance due.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting the label. An unlabelled proforma looks exactly like a real invoice and can confuse the client's accounts team — always mark it clearly.
  • Recording it as income. A proforma isn't revenue and shouldn't hit your books until you issue the final invoice. Mixing the two double-counts your earnings.
  • Reusing the same number. Give the final invoice its own sequential number; don't pass the proforma off as the official record.
  • No expiry. Without a "valid until" date, you're bound to quoted prices indefinitely. Add a short validity window.

Frequently asked questions

Is a proforma invoice a real invoice?

No. A proforma invoice is a preliminary document sent before a sale is finalised. It shows the expected cost but isn't a legal demand for payment and isn't recorded in your accounts. The real, numbered invoice is issued once the client confirms and the work or delivery proceeds.

Can you get paid from a proforma invoice?

A client can pay against a proforma — it's common for deposits and prepayments — but the proforma itself isn't the official record of the sale. Once payment or confirmation happens, you should issue a final invoice (and a receipt for any payment received) so your books are correct.

What's the difference between a proforma invoice and a quote?

A quote is an informal estimate of likely cost. A proforma invoice presents that estimate in full invoice format — line items, taxes, terms — so it looks like the bill the client will receive. A proforma is more formal and is often used to request a deposit or get internal cost approval.

Does a proforma invoice need an invoice number?

It doesn't need an official sequential number, and many people just mark it "Proforma" with a draft reference. Save the proper, sequential invoice number for the final invoice that becomes the legal record in your accounts.

Do I charge tax on a proforma invoice?

Show any tax (such as VAT or sales tax) on the proforma exactly as it will appear on the final invoice, so the client sees the true total. But because the proforma isn't a recorded sale, the tax isn't actually due until you issue the real invoice.
