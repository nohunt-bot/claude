How to write a late payment reminder email

An invoice has gone past due and you need the money — but you also need the client. The good news: most late payments are forgetfulness, not refusal. A clear, friendly reminder usually fixes it. Below are four copy-paste templates — from polite nudge to final notice — and exactly when to send each.

When to send each reminder

Don't fire off an angry email the day an invoice is due. Escalate on a schedule instead — the tone hardens as the days stack up, but you always give the client a graceful way to pay.

WhenToneGoal
2–3 days before dueFriendly heads-upOptional. Surfaces the invoice before it's even late.
1 day after duePolite reminderAssume they forgot. Make it easy to pay.
7–14 days after dueFirm follow-upRestate the amount and due date, ask for a payment date.
30+ days after dueFinal noticeState consequences (late fee, pause work) and a hard deadline.

1. The friendly heads-up (before it's due)

Sent a couple of days before the due date, this one is pure goodwill — it reminds a busy client the bill is coming and removes the "I never saw it" excuse later.

Subject: Invoice #1024 — due Friday

Hi [Name],

Quick heads-up that invoice #1024 for [project] is due this Friday, [date]. I've attached it again here for convenience.

Thanks so much — let me know if you need anything from me to process it.

Best,
[Your name]

2. The polite reminder (1 day late)

The day after the due date, lead with the benefit of the doubt. The overwhelming majority of late invoices get paid right here.

Subject: Invoice #1024 — now past due

Hi [Name],

I hope you're well. I wanted to follow up on invoice #1024 for [amount], which was due on [date] and is now showing as unpaid. It may have slipped through — I've re-attached it here.

Could you let me know once it's been scheduled for payment? Happy to resend it in any format your accounts team prefers.

Thanks,
[Your name]

3. The firm follow-up (1–2 weeks late)

If the polite reminder went unanswered, drop the soft framing. Be courteous but direct, and ask for a specific payment date — a concrete question is harder to ignore than a general nudge.

Subject: Second reminder — invoice #1024 ([amount]) overdue

Hi [Name],

This is a second reminder that invoice #1024 for [amount] is now [X] days overdue. The original due date was [date] and I haven't yet received payment or a reply to my last email.

Can you confirm the date the payment will be made? If there's an issue with the invoice or a hold-up on your end, let me know and I'll help sort it out.

I'd appreciate a reply by [date].

Best,
[Your name]

4. The final notice (30+ days late)

By now you're protecting your business. State the consequence plainly — a late fee if your terms allow one, pausing further work, or escalation — and give a firm deadline. Keep it professional; this email may end up forwarded to a manager or used as evidence later.

Subject: Final notice — invoice #1024 overdue [X] days

Hi [Name],

Despite previous reminders, invoice #1024 for [amount], originally due [date], remains unpaid [X] days later.

Please arrange payment in full by [hard deadline]. As noted in our agreed terms, a late fee of [%/amount] applies to overdue balances, and I'll need to pause any further work until the account is settled.

I'd much rather resolve this directly — please reply today to let me know when payment will be made.

Regards,
[Your name]

Tips that make reminders work

  • Attach the invoice every time. Don't make the client dig for it — re-send the PDF with each reminder so paying is one click away.
  • Reference the invoice number and amount. It looks organized and gives the accounts team exactly what they need to find it.
  • Put the due date on the invoice itself. A clearly printed due date prevents most "I didn't know it was late" replies. See Net 30 payment terms explained for how to set and display terms.
  • Keep emotion out of it. Firm is fine; angry is not. Calm, factual reminders get paid and keep the relationship intact.
  • Make it easy to pay. Include your payment details in every reminder so there's no back-and-forth.

Frequently asked questions

How soon after the due date should I send a reminder?

Send the first polite reminder the day after the invoice is due. Most late payments are simple oversights, and a same-week nudge resolves the majority of them before they become a real problem.

How do I ask for payment without sounding rude?

Lead with the benefit of the doubt in early reminders ("it may have slipped through"), keep the tone factual rather than emotional, and make paying easy by attaching the invoice and your payment details. Save firm language for when earlier, friendlier reminders have gone unanswered.

Can I charge a late fee on an overdue invoice?

Only if your agreed terms or original invoice stated one — you can't invent a fee after the fact. The cleanest approach is to print your late-fee policy on the invoice from the start, then reference it in your final notice if you need to.
