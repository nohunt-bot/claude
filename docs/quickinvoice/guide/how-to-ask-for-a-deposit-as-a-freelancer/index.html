How to ask for a deposit as a freelancer

A deposit is the single best protection against doing weeks of work and never getting paid. Asking for money before you start feels awkward the first time — but it's standard, professional, and the clients worth keeping won't blink. Here's how much to ask for, when to request it, the exact words to use, and how to put deposit terms on your invoice and contract.

Why ask for a deposit at all?

An upfront deposit does three things at once. It filters out time-wasters — a client unwilling to pay anything before work begins is the client most likely to vanish at invoice time. It covers your early costs and time, so you're not fully exposed if a project stalls halfway. And it signals commitment on both sides: once money has changed hands, a project feels real to the client, not a "maybe."

It also flips the risk. Without a deposit, you front all the work and hope to be paid later. With one, the client has skin in the game from day one.

How much should the deposit be?

There's no legal rule — it's whatever you and the client agree. But a few norms make the conversation easy:

DepositWhen it fits
25–30%The common default for most project work. Big enough to protect you, small enough that clients accept it without negotiation.
50%Larger projects, new clients with no track record, or work with real upfront costs (materials, software, subcontractors). Often split 50% up front, 50% on delivery.
100% up frontSmall fixed jobs, rush work, or clients you don't yet trust. Common for sub-$500 tasks where invoicing twice isn't worth it.
Milestone depositsLong projects: take a deposit, then bill in stages as each milestone is approved, rather than one lump at the end.

If you're not sure what number to anchor to, work back from your rate first — our guide on how much to charge as a freelancer covers setting the project total the deposit is a percentage of.

When to ask — timing is everything

Ask for the deposit after the client says yes but before you start work. That's the moment leverage is highest: they want the project to begin, and the deposit is simply the gate that opens it. The sequence that works:

  1. Agree the scope and total price.
  2. Send a short contract or written scope that states the deposit terms.
  3. Send a deposit invoice for the agreed percentage.
  4. Start work only once the deposit clears — not when it's "promised."

That last point matters. "I'll send it over today" is not a cleared payment. Booking your time before the deposit lands is how freelancers end up doing free work.

The exact words to use

Keep it matter-of-fact. You're not asking a favour — you're describing how you work. Two templates:

Hi [Name], great — I'm glad to take this on. To get started I take a [30%] deposit, with the balance due on delivery. I'll send a deposit invoice now and book you in as soon as it's cleared. Looking forward to it!

Hi [Name], thanks for confirming. My standard terms are [50%] up front and [50%] on completion. I've attached the deposit invoice — once that's paid I'll get started and share the first draft by [date].

Notice what these don't do: they don't apologise, over-explain, or ask permission. State the terms as a normal part of starting, and most clients simply pay.

How to put it on the invoice

A deposit invoice is just a normal invoice for part of the total. Make it unmistakable:

  • Label the line item "Deposit (30% of project total)" so it's clear this isn't the full fee.
  • Note the remaining balance and when it's due — e.g. "Balance of $700 due on delivery."
  • Give it a real invoice number and a due date, like any invoice.
  • When the project finishes, send a second invoice for the balance, referencing the deposit already paid.

Put deposit terms in the contract too

The invoice requests the money; the contract is what makes the arrangement stick. Even a one-page agreement should spell out the deposit so there's no argument later. Cover:

ClauseWhat to state
Deposit amountThe percentage or fixed sum, and that work begins once it's received.
Non-refundable?Whether the deposit is refundable if the client cancels. Many freelancers make it non-refundable to cover booked time.
Balance & termsWhen the rest is due and on what terms (e.g. Net 30 or on delivery).
Late paymentWhat happens if the balance is late — pausing work, a late fee, etc.

A deposit clause and clear payment terms are the two lines that prevent most freelance payment disputes before they start.

What if the client says no?

Some will push back. Hold the line calmly:

  • "It's just how I work." Framing it as standard policy, not a personal demand, defuses most objections.
  • Offer a smaller first step. If 50% scares a new client, propose a paid discovery phase or a 25% deposit instead.
  • Read the refusal as data. A client who won't pay anything up front is showing you exactly how the final invoice will go. Sometimes "no deposit" is your cue to walk away.

And if a balance does go unpaid later, deposit or not, you'll want a late-payment reminder email ready to send.

Frequently asked questions

How much deposit should a freelancer ask for?

25–30% is the common default for most project work — large enough to protect your time, small enough that clients accept it easily. Move to 50% for larger projects, new clients, or work with real upfront costs, and consider 100% up front for small or rush jobs.

Is it normal to ask for a deposit before starting work?

Yes. Taking a deposit before beginning is standard freelance practice. It protects you from unpaid work, confirms the client is serious, and covers your early time and costs. Professional clients expect it and won't be put off.

When should I ask for the deposit?

Ask after the client has agreed to the project but before you start work. Send a deposit invoice for the agreed percentage and begin only once the payment has actually cleared — not when it's been promised.

Should the deposit be refundable?

That's your call, but many freelancers make deposits non-refundable to cover time they've booked and turned other work away for. Whatever you decide, state it clearly in your contract so there's no dispute if the client cancels.

How do I invoice a deposit?

Create a normal invoice for the deposit amount, label the line item clearly as a deposit (e.g. "Deposit — 30% of project total"), note the remaining balance and when it's due, and give it an invoice number. Send a second invoice for the balance when the work is delivered.
